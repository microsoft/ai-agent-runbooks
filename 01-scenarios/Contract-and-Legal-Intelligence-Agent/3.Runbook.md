# Step-by-Step Guide — Contract & Legal Intelligence Agent

> **Platform**: Copilot Studio · Foundry / Document Intelligence · SharePoint · Word
> **Target Readers**: Delivery engineer, solution architect
> **Estimated effort**: 6–8 weeks; standard-position definition dominates the front end

## Phase 0 — Boundary and Standard Positions (Weeks 1–2)

### Step 0-1. Sign the boundary statement

One page, drafted by you and edited and signed by legal:

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| What the agent does | "Extracts clauses and obligations, compares them against <Organisation>'s documented standard positions, and flags deviations with reasoning and source references, for review by a qualified person." |
| What it never does | "Does not provide legal advice. Does not determine enforceability, validity, or legal effect. Does not approve, execute, or send any agreement. Produces no communication to a counterparty without human review." |
| Who reviews | Named role, qualified |
| What the record is | "The reviewed and committed output is the record. The agent's draft is not." |

**Nothing else starts until this is signed.**

### Step 0-2. Produce the standard positions

This is a legal work package. Facilitate it; do not attempt it yourself.

## Phase 2 — Build the Analysis Agent (Week 4)

Instruction block:

```text
ROLE
You analyse contracts for <Organisation> by comparing them against documented
standard positions. You are not a lawyer and you do not give legal advice.

HARD BOUNDARIES — these override everything else
- Never state whether a clause is enforceable, valid, or legally effective.
- Never recommend whether to sign, reject, or proceed.
- Never advise on legal strategy or likely outcomes of a dispute.
- Never produce communication to a counterparty for sending. Drafts only,
  clearly labelled, for human review.
- If asked to do any of the above: "That requires legal judgement. I can show
  you the relevant clauses and how they compare to our standard position."

METHOD
For each clause type in the standard position library:
1. Locate the corresponding clause in the document. Quote it verbatim.
2. Record its location (section, page).
3. Compare it against the standard position.
4. Classify: MATCHES STANDARD | ACCEPTABLE FALLBACK | DEVIATION | RED LINE |
   ABSENT
5. For anything other than MATCHES STANDARD, state:
   - exactly what differs
   - the risk category and severity from the taxonomy
   - the reasoning, in terms of the taxonomy — not general legal opinion

RULES
- Every finding must quote the source clause and give its location.
- If a clause is absent, say ABSENT. Never infer intent from silence.
- If the document is unreadable in part, say which part. Never fill gaps.
- Do not comment on clause types outside the standard position library.
- Record which VERSION of the standard positions you compared against.

OUTPUT
A structured finding list. Then a summary counting each classification.
Everything is a DRAFT for qualified review.
```

The classification scheme matters more than the prose. Five discrete states are reviewable; a
free-text risk assessment is not.

## Phase 3 — Redline Generation (Week 5)

Only if redlining is in scope.

| Requirement | Verified |
|---|---|
| Output is **tracked changes on the original**, not a regenerated document | |
| Original formatting, numbering, and cross-references preserved | |
| Every change carries a rationale in the change summary | |
| PDF input produces a clearly-labelled Word rendition | |
| Draft counterparty response is a separate artefact | |
| Nothing is sent automatically | |

Test on a heavily formatted contract with nested numbering. Mangled numbering destroys reviewer
trust faster than a wrong risk flag.

## Phase 4 — Evaluate (Week 6)

### Step 4-1. Accuracy against a lawyer baseline

Have a qualified reviewer analyse 15 contracts independently. Compare.

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Clauses correctly located | ≥95% |
| Classification agreement with the reviewer | ≥85% |
| **Deviations missed (false negatives)** | **Near zero — the critical measure** |
| False positives | Tolerable; the reviewer dismisses them |
| Every finding has a valid quote and location | 100% |
| **Any output constituting legal advice** | **0 — hard fail** |

A missed red line is the failure that matters. Tune for recall on deviations, and accept false
positives as the cost.

### Step 4-2. Boundary review by legal

Give legal 10 real outputs and one instruction: **find anything that reads as legal advice.** This
review determines whether the agent goes live.

### Step 4-3. Consistency

Run the same contract three times. Classifications must match. Inconsistency here usually means the
standard positions are ambiguous, not that the model is unstable.

---

## Phase 5 — Pilot and Rollout

1. Pilot on **one contract type** with one reviewer group.
2. Run in parallel with manual review for a full cycle.
3. Measure review time per contract against the baseline.

| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| Zero advice-boundary failures, missed deviations near zero, review time down | Proceed |
| Review time unchanged | Fix the review surface before scaling |
| **Any advice-boundary failure** | Stop. Revise, re-test, re-review with legal |
| Missed red lines | Stop. This is the one failure the scenario cannot carry |

Expand one contract type at a time. Each type needs its own standard positions and its own
accuracy baseline.

---

## Phase 6 — Operate

| Cadence | Activity | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly (first month) | Review findings for advice-boundary drift | Legal |
| Weekly | Missed-deviation analysis | Delivery engineer |
| Monthly | Review time and throughput vs baseline | Legal ops |
| On standard position change | Re-version and re-baseline | Legal + delivery |
| On new contract type | Full Phase 0–4 cycle | Delivery engineer |
| Quarterly | Re-run the evaluation set | Delivery engineer |

Standard positions change. Record which version each analysis used, and re-baseline when they move.
